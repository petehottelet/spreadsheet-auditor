## Notes by rule

- **BLANK_PRECEDENT**: Was 15,300 findings at 4% precision on 0.2.0 (guarded formulas, template rows, mirror links, blank-ignoring aggregates); positional and guarded uses, blank-tolerant functions, rows with no inputs and sparse columns were then excluded and 29 findings remained, none of them a defect: a ledger with paired amount columns (debit or credit, buying or selling) where the blank is a zero by design, plus empty rows inside a block. Since 0.4.0 the rule is off by default and does not run on this corpus; turn it on for data that must never have gaps.
- **BROKEN_REFERENCE**: Right every time: #REF! formulas, and external links now reported as one Info note per source workbook per sheet (was one High finding per cell).
- **CIRCULAR_REFERENCE**: Positional idioms (ROWS(A$6:A8), COLUMNS($D7:AA7), ROW(A19)) and the CELL("filename",Q2) sheet-name idiom were 23 of 25 findings on 0.2.0 and are gone. What remains is the deliberate IFERROR(1/(1/F8)) date-freeze self-reference (which needs iterative calculation) and a MAX(B$7:B377)+1 counter that includes its own cell; both are worth a reviewer's attention.
- **DUPLICATE_KEY**: Only the searched column of a first-match lookup counts (VLOOKUP first column, MATCH/XLOOKUP lookup array), one finding per searched column since 0.4.0, and error values such as #N/A are not keys. True positives are duplicate postcodes in a MATCH column, product names listed twice in a list searched by name, letters listed twice in a rate table, and a lookup that finds the oldest sale where the sheet wants the latest. The false positives dominate the 0.4.0 sample: composite keys whose match is decided by another column (group and material, material and validity dates, category by region, day label by date), product labels repeated as section headers over quantities in one family of bay-map grids (several copies of it in the sample), rows of data repeated per ID, and lookups that deliberately find the previous occurrence. The next refinement is to recognise a composite key: a lookup whose other criteria or neighbouring key column tell the rows apart.
- **FORMULA_DRIFT**: 0 of 25 on 0.2.0. Rows of unrelated columns, chain seeds, label-link columns that step through another sheet, and short or header-labelled totals rows mixing SUM and AVERAGE are exempt; a SUM that became an AVERAGE inside a longer run of totals is still reported, because that is the 'function replaced' fault the EUSES corpus injects. What remains is mostly real: a first row whose SUMPRODUCT differs from the rows below and evaluates to #VALUE!, a DB() call missing an argument, a typed threshold of 90120 where the rows around use 90, a total that starts one row late, a typed input inside a fill (=525*1.5 beside =BF24*1.5), and a manual override link. Summary cells that are not part of the run beside them are exempt too: a link heading a column of labels (L2 = M14 over a column of names, where the run's SUM would add up text) and one of a row of counts over one block (COUNTIF(F5:F11,1), 2 and 3). Of the six findings this removed, the four already labeled were FP or unsure, and EUSES recall did not move. Remaining false positives are summary or totals rows whose different formula is the design (a ratio of totals, a 'planned' row that averages under rows that sum, a wins row that counts under an 'average' header) and example sheets where every cell is a different formula. In the 0.4.0 sample two false positives compute exactly what their neighbours do in another spelling ($-anchored references to the same cells, SUM(x) around a product); comparing the cells a formula resolves to would remove them.
- **HARDCODE_IN_FORMULA_BLOCK**: 8% on 0.2.0: input columns between two formula columns sharing a pattern (running sums, unit x quantity). Those are now recognised from the other axis, and the true positives dominate: a constant typed over an IF, a difference, a rolling sum or a scaled copy in a column of identical formulas. Remaining false positives are input columns on sparse sheets or one-row tables, where the cells above and below the constant are empty and the check has nothing to compare, and input columns whose neighbouring formula lines hold plugs of their own. In the 0.4.0 sample the four false positives are the Actual input columns of one budget workbook family.
- **HIDDEN_STRUCTURE_IN_TOTAL**: Hidden helper columns feeding scores, hidden rows still counted by SUMIFS, hidden lookup tables and hidden source sheets: every sampled finding was a disclosure a reviewer wants. AGGREGATE and SUBTOTAL variants that skip hidden rows no longer count, and hidden rows feeding the same formulas are reported together.
- **IFERROR_MASK**: Since 0.4.0 a wrapper is reported only when its expression can really fail (a division, a lookup, an average, a text or date conversion, arithmetic on a cell) and the fallback reads as a real value; IFNA counts only what returns #N/A, and errors an ISNUMBER or inner IFERROR absorbs, conversions that fall back to the text they read, second lookups, messages and NA() are skipped. That took the rule from 1,763 findings at 28% to 644 at 60%. True positives: Google Sheets placeholder formulas, lookups that turn a missing age, stay or date into 0, averages and ratios that show 0 when there is nothing to average, and a payment schedule. False positives: one order-sheet layout where a site missing from a lookup really is 0 units (several copies in the sample), ranked lists whose end is a blank, and a 0 returned into a text column such as Gender, which the rule cannot tell from a number column.
- **LITERAL_CONSTANT**: Function-slot literals (column indexes, type codes, string positions, date parts, criteria) and the unit constants 60/3600 are skipped, and one finding per formula pattern replaces one per cell. Since 0.4.0 the value a rounding or formatting function works on is checked too (ROUND(A1*1.0725,2) reports 1.0725, not the 2), and a 3 or 6 dividing MONTH() is a calendar fact. True positives are thresholds and assumptions: 37.5 hours, a 90-day cutoff, bonus caps and rates, minimum sample sizes, lead times, pay multipliers, payment terms. One >=10 minimum-sample threshold repeated across many cells with different criteria counts as many patterns. Remaining false positives are calendar grid offsets, ROW() comparisons, literals that mirror a column header, RANDBETWEEN bounds, a MATCH()+3 row offset, and codes returned by IF. The count rose from 1,015 in 0.3.0 because the old report cap had hidden these findings in workbooks flooded by live errors.
- **LIVE_ERROR**: Every sampled finding was a real error value: #REF! formulas, lookups missing their keys, #VALUE! from external links or text arithmetic, #DIV/0! averages with nothing to average, array formulas entered without array evaluation, and error constants pasted into data. Since 0.4.0 an error filled down a column is one finding listing its cells, and the cells an error cycle feeds are listed with it; a #REF! can also be reported by BROKEN_REFERENCE on the same cell.
- **MERGED_CELL_IN_DATA_RANGE**: 0 of 25 on 0.2.0 (titles, headers, labels, merged dates whose formulas already fetch the last non-blank value) and 0 of 24 after the first pass (merged title formulas, merged net lines in totals rows, merged weekly limits). Only a merged number read through a range reference now counts; the rule no longer fires on this corpus and keeps its seeded case.
- **NUMBERS_STORED_AS_TEXT**: The 'referenced by a formula' variant needs a numeric consumer and skips leading-zero identifiers and codes that are only compared; since 0.4.0 it is one finding per column, and text numbers that every formula converts first (--A1, VALUE, *1) are a Low note. True positives are ID and count columns that mix text and numeric forms, a pasted amount with a non-breaking space, and batch numbers in both forms. The false positives were free-text notes columns holding a few bare numbers.
- **RANGE_EXCLUSION**: Four true positives (a total whose siblings span all rows while it stops early); the running COUNT($A$9:A9) false positives are exempt.
- **RANGE_INCLUDES_SUBTOTAL**: The three 0.2.0 findings were a carried-forward total; the constants variant was dropped and the rule no longer fires on this corpus. Its seeded double-count case still passes.
- **RANGE_LENGTH_MISMATCH**: Every remaining finding is COUNT(D87:F89) in a column of COUNT(D87:F87) formulas, a real fill error in one family of bowling sheets; one-cell links and disjoint debit/credit groups no longer fire.
- **VOLATILE_FUNCTION**: TODAY/NOW are one Info note per sheet, which is the right weight; RAND/RANDBETWEEN feeding analyses, INDIRECT-built addresses and OFFSET over #REF! are reported per pattern. All worth a look at their level.
- **WHITESPACE_KEY**: 8% on 0.2.0 (fixed-width export padding, indentation, headers, spacer cells). Export-padded columns get one Info note, headers and indentation are skipped, and since 0.4.0 the stray padded keys of a column are one finding that names them all. True positives: a padded name among clean ones, a status with a trailing space beside the formula that compares it, leading spaces on postcodes that break a URN match, a trailing newline that turns a duration into #VALUE!. Remaining false positives: header cells below the first rows ("Grade ", "Week "), report dumps with fixed-width text, captions and prose, and uniformly padded account names in a short list.
- **WHOLE_COLUMN_REFERENCE**: COUNTIF/SUMIF/VLOOKUP/MATCH/INDEX over whole columns are bounded by Excel and no longer fire, even inside array formulas. Every remaining finding is an array evaluation over a million rows: IF(A2=ZORD!A:A,...), MAX(IF(F:F=...)), LOOKUP(2,1/(J:J<>"")), SUMPRODUCT over whole columns.

## Method and caveats

- Every workbook is audited with the CLI in a subprocess with a per-file timeout; crashes and timeouts are counted, not hidden.
- The sample takes up to 25 findings per rule, at most 2 from any one workbook, seeded, so a rule that fires thousands of times is judged on the same footing as one that fires ten times.
- Each sampled finding is judged from a context card (formula, cached value, row labels, column header, a 5x5 neighbourhood, and the values inside and just outside the first referenced range). TP means the finding points at something a careful reviewer would want to look at for the reason the rule gives; FP means the rule misread the workbook; unsure means the card was not enough to tell.
- Precision is TP / (TP + FP) with a Wilson 95% interval. Small samples have wide intervals; read the interval, not the point estimate.
- This measures precision only. Recall needs ground truth; see the `euses-modified` source in `benchmarks/corpus/sources.json`.
- Forum workbooks are small and question-shaped, so volumes per 1k formulas will not transfer to large financial models; the per-rule precision is the more portable number.
