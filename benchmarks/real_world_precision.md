## Notes by rule

- **BLANK_PRECEDENT**: Was 15,300 findings at 4% precision on 0.2.0 (guarded formulas, template rows, mirror links, blank-ignoring aggregates). Positional and guarded uses, blank-tolerant functions, rows with no inputs and sparse columns are now excluded and 29 findings remain. What is left is one shape: a ledger with paired amount columns (debit or credit, buying or selling, trade size or none) where the blank is a zero by design, plus empty rows inside a block. None of the remaining sample was a defect; the rule is now rare enough to leave, and the next refinement would be to skip a blank whose row holds a filled sibling amount column.
- **BROKEN_REFERENCE**: Right every time: #REF! formulas, and external links now reported as one Info note per source workbook per sheet (was one High finding per cell).
- **CIRCULAR_REFERENCE**: Positional idioms (ROWS(A$6:A8), COLUMNS($D7:AA7), ROW(A19)) and the CELL("filename",Q2) sheet-name idiom were 23 of 25 findings on 0.2.0 and are gone. What remains is the deliberate IFERROR(1/(1/F8)) date-freeze self-reference (which needs iterative calculation) and a MAX(B$7:B377)+1 counter that includes its own cell; both are worth a reviewer's attention.
- **DUPLICATE_KEY**: Only the searched column of a first-match lookup counts now (VLOOKUP first column, MATCH/XLOOKUP lookup array); SUMIF criteria columns and category grids no longer fire. True positives are duplicate postcodes in a MATCH column, duplicate product names in a list searched by name, and letters listed twice in a rate table. The remaining false positives are lookups that deliberately find the previous occurrence, composite keys where the match is on another column, and product labels repeated as section headers in a grid.
- **FORMULA_DRIFT**: 0 of 25 on 0.2.0. Rows of unrelated columns, chain seeds, label-link columns that step through another sheet, and totals rows mixing SUM and AVERAGE are now exempt. What remains is mostly real: a first row whose SUMPRODUCT differs from the rows below and evaluates to #VALUE!, a DB() call missing an argument, a total that starts one row late, a typed input inside a fill (=525*1.5 beside =BF24*1.5), and a manual override link. Remaining false positives are summary header rows and example sheets where every cell is a different formula.
- **HARDCODE_IN_FORMULA_BLOCK**: 8% on 0.2.0: input columns between two formula columns sharing a pattern (running sums, unit x quantity). Those are now recognised from the other axis, and the true positives dominate: a constant typed over an IF, a difference, a rolling sum or a scaled copy in a column of identical formulas. Remaining false positives are input columns on sparse sheets or one-row tables, where the cells above and below the constant are empty and the check has nothing to compare, and input columns whose neighbouring formula lines hold plugs of their own.
- **HIDDEN_STRUCTURE_IN_TOTAL**: Hidden helper columns feeding scores, hidden rows still counted by SUMIFS, hidden lookup tables and hidden source sheets: every sampled finding was a disclosure a reviewer wants. AGGREGATE and SUBTOTAL variants that skip hidden rows no longer count, and hidden rows feeding the same formulas are reported together.
- **IFERROR_MASK**: Disclosure rule, now one finding per formula pattern per sheet. Judged TP when the fallback silently replaces a computational error with a value that feeds other cells: a lookup miss shown as an age or length of stay of 0, an average of 0 for no trades, IFNA(...,0) on an input sheet, a sliding VLOOKUP range whose misses are hidden, an exact-match tier lookup that can never match, and Google Sheets placeholder formulas. FP for the lookup-miss-to-blank idiom, end-of-list extraction, supplier fallback chains, and wrappers around functions that cannot error or whose inner IF already guards the case. Precision stays under a third; the rule is honest about being a review prompt.
- **LITERAL_CONSTANT**: Function-slot literals (column indexes, type codes, string positions, date parts, criteria) and the unit constants 60/3600 are now skipped, and one finding per formula pattern replaces one per cell. True positives are thresholds and assumptions: 37.5 hours, a 90-day cutoff, bonus caps and rates, minimum sample sizes, lead times, arithmetic on two typed numbers. Remaining false positives are calendar grid offsets, ROW() comparisons, literals that mirror a column header, RANDBETWEEN bounds, and codes returned by IF.
- **LIVE_ERROR**: Every sampled finding was a real error value: #REF! formulas, lookups missing their keys, #VALUE! from external links or text arithmetic, array formulas entered without array evaluation, and error constants pasted into data. Grouping by root cause keeps the volume sane.
- **MERGED_CELL_IN_DATA_RANGE**: 0 of 25 on 0.2.0 (titles, headers, labels, merged dates whose formulas already fetch the last non-blank value) and 0 of 24 after the first pass (merged title formulas, merged net lines in totals rows, merged weekly limits). Only a merged number read through a range reference now counts; the rule no longer fires on this corpus and keeps its seeded case.
- **NUMBERS_STORED_AS_TEXT**: The 'referenced by a formula' variant now needs a numeric consumer and skips leading-zero identifiers and codes that are only compared; the numeric-column variant is unchanged. True positives are ID and count columns that mix text and numeric forms, a pasted amount with a non-breaking space, and batch numbers in both forms. The one false positive was a free-text notes column holding a few bare numbers.
- **RANGE_EXCLUSION**: Four true positives (a total whose siblings span all rows while it stops early); the running COUNT($A$9:A9) false positives are exempt.
- **RANGE_INCLUDES_SUBTOTAL**: The three 0.2.0 findings were a carried-forward total; the constants variant was dropped and the rule no longer fires on this corpus. Its seeded double-count case still passes.
- **RANGE_LENGTH_MISMATCH**: Every remaining finding is COUNT(D87:F89) in a column of COUNT(D87:F87) formulas, a real fill error in one family of bowling sheets; one-cell links and disjoint debit/credit groups no longer fire.
- **VOLATILE_FUNCTION**: TODAY/NOW are one Info note per sheet, which is the right weight; RAND/RANDBETWEEN feeding analyses, INDIRECT-built addresses and OFFSET over #REF! are reported per pattern. All worth a look at their level.
- **WHITESPACE_KEY**: 8% on 0.2.0 (fixed-width export padding, indentation, headers, spacer cells). Export-padded columns get one Info note, headers and indentation are skipped, and what remains is the stray padded key: one padded name among clean ones, a status with a trailing space beside the formula that compares it, leading spaces on postcodes that break the URN match, a trailing newline that turns a duration into #VALUE!. Remaining false positives are report dumps with fixed-width text and uniformly padded account names in a short list.
- **WHOLE_COLUMN_REFERENCE**: COUNTIF/SUMIF/VLOOKUP/MATCH/INDEX over whole columns are bounded by Excel and no longer fire, even inside array formulas. Every remaining finding is an array evaluation over a million rows: IF(A2=ZORD!A:A,...), MAX(IF(F:F=...)), LOOKUP(2,1/(J:J<>"")), SUMPRODUCT over whole columns.

## Method and caveats

- Every workbook is audited with the CLI in a subprocess with a per-file timeout; crashes and timeouts are counted, not hidden.
- The sample takes up to 25 findings per rule, at most 2 from any one workbook, seeded, so a rule that fires thousands of times is judged on the same footing as one that fires ten times.
- Each sampled finding is judged from a context card (formula, cached value, row labels, column header, a 5x5 neighbourhood, and the values inside and just outside the first referenced range). TP means the finding points at something a careful reviewer would want to look at for the reason the rule gives; FP means the rule misread the workbook; unsure means the card was not enough to tell.
- Precision is TP / (TP + FP) with a Wilson 95% interval. Small samples have wide intervals; read the interval, not the point estimate.
- This measures precision only. Recall needs ground truth; see the `euses-modified` source in `benchmarks/corpus/sources.json`.
- Forum workbooks are small and question-shaped, so volumes per 1k formulas will not transfer to large financial models; the per-rule precision is the more portable number.
